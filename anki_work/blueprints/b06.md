## 06a–06b Lectures 16–17 — Image processing (cram)
1. [high] What is discrete convolution at a pixel? (study guide; L16 p.6, 15)
   Full-mark: weighted sum of neighbourhood intensities using a kernel; centred neighbourhoods are odd-sized.
2. [high] Why must smoothing kernels sum to 1? What if a \(3\times 3\) mean forgets \(/9\)? (study guide; practice Q4; L16 p.12)
   Full-mark: preserves mean intensity / avoids brightness bias; without \(/9\) image ~9× brighter and clips to 255.
3. [high] Border strategies: copy, image truncation, kernel truncation, reflection — effect on size/border. (study guide; L16 p.22–25)
   Full-mark: copy → same size, unprocessed border; truncate image → smaller/offset; truncate kernel → modified edge kernels; reflection → mirror coords (most useful).
4. [high] Unsharp masking formulas \(g=f-S(f)\) and \(f_{UM}=f+kg\). (study guide; L17 p.10–11)
   Full-mark: \(g\) is edge image (original minus blur); add \(kg\) back; \(k\) controls strength (~0.2–0.7; \(k>1\) high-boost).
5. [high] Contrast Gaussian vs median filtering. (study guide; L17 p.27–30, 35–38)
   Full-mark: Gaussian = nonuniform LPF from \(e^{-(i^2+j^2)/(2\sigma^2)}\), larger \(\sigma\) more blur, good for Gaussian noise; median = order-statistic middle value, kills impulse noise (< half neighbourhood), preserves edges better than mean.
