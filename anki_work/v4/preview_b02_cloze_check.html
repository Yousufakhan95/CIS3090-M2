Front:
You time the serial program and the parallel program on the same input. Speedup is \(S =\) [...].
Back:
You time the serial program and the parallel program on the same input. Speedup is \(S =\) \(\frac{T_{\text{serial} } }{T_{\text{parallel} } }\).
A speedup of 4 means the parallel version finishes in a quarter of the serial time.

Efficiency is the speedup you get per core, \(E =\) [...] \(=\) \(\frac{T_{\text{serial} } }{p \cdot T_{\text{parallel} } }\).
Back:
Efficiency is the speedup you get per core, \(E =\) \(\frac{S}{p}\) \(=\) \(\frac{T_{\text{serial} } }{p \cdot T_{\text{parallel} } }\).
Linear speedup gives \(E = 1\). Lecture 14 writes the first fraction as \(S/P\) with a capital P, but P and \(p\) are both the core count.

Front:
A program takes 100 s on 1 core, 40 s on 4 cores and 25 s on 8 cores. Speedup grows from [...] to [...], and efficiency drops from 0.625 to 0.5.
Back:
A program takes 100 s on 1 core, 40 s on 4 cores and 25 s on 8 cores. Speedup grows from 2.5 to 4, and efficiency drops from 0.625 to 0.5.
\(S = 100/40 = 2.5\) and \(E = 2.5/4\). \(S = 100/25 = 4\) and \(E = 4/8\). This is study guide practice question 5.

Lecture 14 lists the overheads that slow a parallel program. For threads, they are managing the threads, [...], and calls to mutex functions.
Back:
Lecture 14 lists the overheads that slow a parallel program. For threads, they are managing the threads, critical sections, and calls to mutex functions.
A mutex makes the threads run the critical section one at a time. A serial program has none of these costs.

Front:
Lecture 14 writes parallel run-time as \(T_{\text{parallel} } =\) [...] \(+\) \(T_{\text{overhead} }\).
Back:
Lecture 14 writes parallel run-time as \(T_{\text{parallel} } =\) \(\frac{T_{\text{serial} } }{p}\) \(+\) \(T_{\text{overhead} }\).
The first term is the ideal time with the work split evenly. The second is the added time to manage the parallel operations.

b02 #9 · tags: b02_performance, amdahl_law · showing cloze c1
Card 9: cloze(s) are outside math — blue highlight should show normally.
Front:
With serial fraction \(a\) on \(p\) cores, Amdahl's law gives \(S =\) [...].
Back:
With serial fraction \(a\) on \(p\) cores, Amdahl's law gives \(S =\) \(\frac{1}{(1-a)/p + a}\).
This is the study guide's form. \(1-a\) is the parallelizable portion. The textbook calls the serial fraction \(r\) instead of \(a\).
b02 #10 · tags: b02_performance, amdahl_limit · showing cloze c1
Card 10: cloze(s) are outside math — blue highlight should show normally.
Front:
As \(p\) keeps growing, the Amdahl speedup approaches [...], where \(a\) is the inherently serial fraction of the work.
Back:
As \(p\) keeps growing, the Amdahl speedup approaches \(1/a\), where \(a\) is the inherently serial fraction of the work.
The \((1-a)/p\) term goes to 0, so only \(a\) is left in the denominator. Past some point, extra cores add almost nothing.
b02 #11 · tags: b02_performance, amdahl_limit · showing cloze c1
Card 11: cloze(s) are outside math — blue highlight should show normally.
Front:
A program that is 90% parallelizable can never get a speedup above [...], even on 1000 cores. At 95% the cap is 20, and at 99% it is 100.
Back:
A program that is 90% parallelizable can never get a speedup above 10, even on 1000 cores. At 95% the cap is 20, and at 99% it is 100.
The cap is \(1/a\), so \(1/0.1 = 10\), \(1/0.05 = 20\) and \(1/0.01 = 100\). A program that is 50% parallelizable caps at 2.
b02 #12 · tags: b02_performance, amdahl_example · showing cloze c1
Card 12: cloze(s) are outside math — blue highlight should show normally.
Front:
A program takes 20 s serially, and 90% of it parallelizes perfectly. On \(p\) cores it takes \(T_{\text{parallel} } =\) [...] seconds, so it never runs faster than 2 seconds.
Back:
A program takes 20 s serially, and 90% of it parallelizes perfectly. On \(p\) cores it takes \(T_{\text{parallel} } =\) \(18/p + 2\) seconds, so it never runs faster than 2 seconds.
The parallel part is \(0.9 \times 20 / p\) and the serial part is \(0.1 \times 20\). The 2-second serial part gives the cap \(S \le 20/2 = 10\).
b02 #13 · tags: b02_performance, amdahl_law · showing cloze c1
Card 13: cloze(s) are outside math — blue highlight should show normally.
Front:
Lecture 14 says Amdahl's law rules out [...] speedup, at least as a result of parallelization. With an inherently serial portion of 0, the law gives \(S =\) \(p\).
Back:
Lecture 14 says Amdahl's law rules out super-linear speedup, at least as a result of parallelization. With an inherently serial portion of 0, the law gives \(S =\) \(p\).
This follows from the formula. \((1-a)/p + a\) is never smaller than \(1/p\), so \(S\) never exceeds \(p\).
