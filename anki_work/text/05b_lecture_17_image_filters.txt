===== PAGE 4 =====
Filters
•Filtering is a fundamental operation that we will use
•Low-pass ﬁlter (LPF): image smoothing (de-noising)
•High-pass ﬁlter (HPF): diﬀerence between image and low-pass ﬁltering.
•High-boost ﬁlter: part of high-pass added to image.
•Low- and High- refer to ﬁlter action in frequency domain.
•e.g. Low-pass means that we "pass" low-frequency components
•So we can call it a "high-cut ﬁlter"
•Large, sudden - i.e. closely spaced - ﬂuctuations in pixel intensity are removed
•These can be undesirable (noise) or desirable (ﬁne detail) 

===== PAGE 5 =====
Image sharpening
•Image sharpening relies on high-pass ﬁltering, which allows high spatial frequencies to 
remain unchanged, but suppresses low frequencies
•A high-pass ﬁlter has the eﬀect of preserving sudden variations in intensity, such as those 
that occur at the boundaries of objects, but suppressing more gradual variations.

===== PAGE 7 =====
Simple sharpening
•How it works:
•When the kernel is over an area of constant or slow-changing intensity values the result of the 
convolution is zero (or small).
•When the intensity values vary rapidly within a neighbourhood, the result of the convolution is a 
large number.
•Caveats of sharpening
•Image sharpening makes noise more prominent - noise has a strong high-frequency component
•In practice, we often want to remove the noise before sharpening

===== PAGE 8 =====
Unsharp masking
•Introduced by Schreiber in 1970 for improving the quality of wirephoto pictures for 
newspapers. 
•Based on the principle of photographic masking whereby a low-contrast positive 
transparency is made of the original negative.
•The mask is then “sandwiched” with the negative and the amalgam used to produce the ﬁnal 
print.
Schreiber, W.F., “Wirephoto quality improvement by unsharp masking”, Pattern Recognition, 1970, V.2, pp.117-121.

Unsharp masking
•Unsharp masking relies on subtracting from an image a “blurred” version of that image

•A common way of sharpening an image.
•Sometimes called a high-boost ﬁlter, if the parameter k (which we see later) is > 1.
•Unlike most of the previously discussed methods, it is a multi-step approach

===== PAGE 21 =====
De-noising (noise suppression) algorithms
•There are many diﬀerent types of noise suppression algorithms.
•Generally, noise suppression ﬁlters are various types of smoothing (low-pass) ﬁlters
•Two primary categories: mean-based and order-statistic based ﬁlters

===== PAGE 22 =====
Averaging = smoothing = noise suppression
•The simplest way to get rid of noise is to perform some form of neighbourhood averaging. 
•Image smoothing, or low-pass ﬁltering.
•Known as mean, sliding mean, or box ﬁlter.
•It has the eﬀect of smoothing or blurring the image, reducing noise but obscuring ﬁne detail
•Essentially, it is the opposite of sharpening

===== PAGE 23 =====
Image smoothing
•The ﬁlter replaces the value of every pixel in an image by the average of the intensity values in 
the neighbourhood deﬁned by the kernel.
•The resulting image has reduced “sharp” transitions in the intensity values.
•Noise consists of sharp transitions ➾ noise reduction
•Edges are also characterized by sharp transitions in intensities, so smoothing ﬁlters blur 
edges.

===== PAGE 30 =====
Gaussian filtering
•Gaussian characteristics:
•More weight is given to central pixels than to those in the periphery of the neighbourhood
•Large values of σ produce a broader spread ➾ increased blurring
•As σ increases the dimensions of the kernel also increase
•The kernel is rotationally symmetric, so there is no directional bias in the amount of smoothing

===== PAGE 34 =====
Rank filtering
•Non-linear ﬁlters known collectively as order statistic ﬁlters or rank ﬁlters 
•These include the median value, lower and upper quartiles, etc..
•Compile a list of intensity values in the neighbourhood of a given pixel, sort this list into 
ascending order
•Then select a value from a particular position in the list to use as the new value for the pixel.

===== PAGE 35 =====
Median filtering
•Select the middle-ranked value (median) from a neighbourhood to replace the pixel (i, j)
•Used to eliminate impulse noise
•Median ﬁltering removes impulse noise eﬀectively when the noisy pixels occupy less than half 
the neighbourhood - the median then comes from the "clean" majority.
•The same mechanism cuts both ways: genuine small features (thin lines, small objects) that 
occupy less than half the neighbourhood are removed too, not just noise.
•Median-ﬁltering is non-speciﬁc
•Any structure that occupies less than half of the ﬁlter's neighbourhood will tend to be eliminated
